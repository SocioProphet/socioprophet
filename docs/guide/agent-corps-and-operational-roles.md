# Agent Corps and Operational Roles

SocioProphet uses a corps of narrowly-scoped operational roles rather than one unconstrained “AI that does everything.”

This is a public-safe summary of those roles.

## 1. Public role model

The public-facing roles include:

- observer and profiler
- egress and policy steward
- safeguard and enforcement role
- provenance and ledger role
- simulation and validation role
- evaluation and diffusion role
- orchestration role
- privacy and DLP role
- bridge and diffusion controller

## 2. Why role separation matters

Role separation reduces risk.

It prevents:

- one component from holding every privilege
- hidden authority chains
- unsafe coupling of detection, enforcement, and promotion
- unreviewable automation drift

## 3. What the corps does publicly

Publicly, we document the corps in terms of:

- mission
- scope
- bounded capabilities
- proof outputs
- safety gates
- operator handoff model

## 4. What we do not publish publicly

We do not publish the full internal operator kit.

We do not publish:

- high-risk playbooks
- sensitive enforcement internals
- adversary-emulation mechanics
- private thresholds
- exploit or persistence methods
- misuse-enabling tactical details

## 5. Relation to the platform

The corps belongs inside the governed operational model described in:

- [Governed AI and Cybernetics](./governed-ai-and-cybernetics)
- [Agent Plane and Operator Workflows](./agent-plane-and-operator-workflows)

## 6. Public takeaway

The point is not to build an army of opaque bots. The point is to create a disciplined, bounded, evidence-producing operational stack with clear roles, proofs, and review.
